How snow rankings work

Snow potential: 1.1 × next 72h snowfall + 1.35 × preserved-snow credit from the past 14 days (snow in inches). Each hourly snowfall layer loses credit gradually with age and faster after warmth, rain and freezing following wet conditions. New snow adds its own credit; it does not reset older layers. This experimental credit is not remaining snow depth or measured powder. Provisional credit deductions: half every 7 days; half per 48 °C-hours above freezing; half per 5 mm of rain/showers; half again when a wet layer freezes. Sun, wind, grooming and skier traffic are not modeled. Opening status is not checked.

Powder Alert: Each incoming day with at least 6″ (15.24 cm, approximately 15 cm) scores snowfall inches × 10 × ½^(days away ÷ 3). The highest-scoring day ranks the resort. Weight halves every three days. Today includes only the still-forecast portion of its hourly intervals, prorating the current hour uniformly. Tile details show the scoring day and the peak incoming day separately. Resort-local dates are used; days 8–16 are longer-range outlooks. Missing/stale forecasts and resorts without a qualifying day are excluded. This is an experimental priority score, not a confidence percentage or opening verification.

Best today: snow from the last 24h, plus half the snow from 24–48h ago and a quarter from 48–72h ago. No future snowfall is counted. Weather deductions cover the past 24h and today’s 9am–4pm hours at the resort, counting each hour once.

Best today starts at 10 points per inch, then applies weather factors: warmth above freezing reduces points by 5% per °F (minimum factor 0.2); rain plus showers applies 1 ÷ (1 + millimeters ÷ 2); wind applies the stronger deduction of 2.5% per mph above 20 mph sustained or 1.67% per mph above 30 mph gusts (minimum factor 0.25); temperatures spanning freezing apply a 0.75 factor. The score is rounded to whole points. Zero snowfall scores zero.

Best today requires a fresh report of open ski runs and complete weather inputs. Biggest forecast ranks the 72-hour snowfall total regardless of opening status; its resort previews show rain, warmth, wind and freeze–thaw tradeoffs when weather inputs are complete. These are experimental snowfall rankings, not validated measures of snow quality. Weather is modeled at one mountain elevation; base coverage, grooming, individual lift closures and forecast uncertainty are not scored.
